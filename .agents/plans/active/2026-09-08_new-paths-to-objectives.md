# New paths to the four objectives (2026-09-08)

**Unverified throughout.** Every number in §2–§5 is a same-day feasibility probe with no null, no knob integration and no leave-one-out. Nothing here is a result. The point of this document is to say which constructions can still reach the objectives, why the old ones could not, and what each new one needs before it may be called a result.

## Head

The old route to "multiscale" was structurally incapable of delivering it, and the two feasibility probes run today say why: on a ~100-node sparse backbone the heat kernel's pair ranking barely changes between s = 1 and s = 200 (effective number of independent scales 1.1–1.5 out of 12), and the one characteristic scale the specific heat shows scatters 3–7× across patients on our working backbone. Whole-vector readouts at different τ were the same statistic wearing different clothes. The construction that escapes this is to take the scale resolution from the diffusion hierarchy itself: bin node pairs by the level of the rest_pre tree at which they merge, so that per-level statistics use disjoint pair sets and are independent by construction. On that construction the β trace is not flat: near zero for pairs joining at the top 2–4 modules, ρ_sym ≈ 0.25–0.30 for pairs joining when 16 or more modules remain, with a within-patient trend in 9/10 patients (p = 0.010) that survives dividing by each level's own split-half reliability and holds across backbone fractions. Encoding vs inference does not separate by level (contrast within ±0.05 at every level), and the literature gives no reason to expect an inference state different in kind from encoding; the objective is re-posed as which block rest_post reinstates after time-equating, with three windowed constructions ranked by how directly they estimate drift. The SOZ marker remains the least-tested and most promising piece, and the level construction gives it a scale axis too.

## 1. The four fundamental issues, and the decision on each

**FC construction.** Keep `imcoh_abs` on the mst-union backbone integrated over f ∈ [0.07, 0.20] (contract). W0-B showed the trace is coherence structure, not lag-specific interaction, so volume-conduction immunity is not its justification; the same-shaft mask (`pair_mask`) is, and every level-resolved statistic below is reported with same-shaft pairs excluded. Bipolar re-referencing is not an option here (it makes the same-shaft bias worse; probe-bias guide §3). One genuinely different FC that is worth one lane as a contrast, not a replacement: orthogonalised amplitude-envelope correlation (Hipp 2012), because it is the coupling mode that carries β resting networks in the literature and a trace present in phase coupling but absent in amplitude coupling (or the reverse) would be a mechanistic statement. It is listed in §7 as optional.

**LRG with τ.** Today's audit (`spectral_range_audit.csv`, 840 cells) fixes what the τ axis can and cannot do on these graphs. Sparse backbones give 2.1–2.4 decades of spectral range but a single dominant specific-heat peak in most cells; the heat kernel's pair ranking has 1.1–1.5 effective independent scales over s ∈ [1, 200]; the peak position is reproducible across patients only for dense, mst0.07 and tmfg (q75/q25 ≈ 1.5–1.8) and scatters 3.4–5.6× for mst0.14 and mst0.20. Backbone density explains 70 % of the variance in these quantities, band 1 %. Consequence: **τ is not a usable independent variable for a cohort claim on this substrate**. The scale coordinate that is usable is the hierarchy level of the diffusion tree, which is comparable across implants when expressed as the number of modules remaining (k) rather than as τ. The LRG still supplies the coordinate system (the tree at s = 1 is the diffusion hierarchy); it stops being asked to supply a τ-resolved readout it cannot give.

**Nulls.** Three rungs stay mandatory for every cohort claim (matched-strength, backbone-fixed N1, order-preserving N3). Two additions: (i) every level-resolved statistic is referenced to the same statistic on the ordered sham, per level, because the finest levels are the strongest pairs and could carry a drift signature of their own; (ii) the ordered-sham ensemble must be deepened beyond two realizations per patient before any margin against drift is called significant. Whole-vector n_eff testing (Lane S) is retired for the level construction because the levels are disjoint by design; the cross-level question becomes a within-patient trend or a coarse-vs-fine paired contrast, both of which cancel the positive bias of ρ_sym.

**Cross-patient aggregation.** Align on k (modules remaining), never on τ or s. Report per-patient profiles and test the profile shape within patients (trend, coarse-vs-fine), then aggregate with Wilcoxon and LOO. For band specificity, replace the single paired test by a repeated-measures design across the six bands (Friedman, then post-hoc), which is what n = 10 can actually power. Pooling pairs across patients into a mixed model is admissible only as a sensitivity analysis, never as the headline, because pair-level dependence within a patient is not modelled by the tools we have.

## 2. Objective 1 — scale-specific on τ, across patients

**Why the old readout could not do it.** Feasibility probe 1 (`feasibility_wavelet_scale.csv`) compared three readouts over s ∈ [0.02, 180]: the cophenetic vector, the heat kernel itself, and the kernel's log-derivative (its graph wavelet, the band-pass object of spectral graph wavelet theory). Below s ≈ 2 all three are identical to the raw FC (split-half reliability constant at 0.72 α / 0.80 β from s = 0.02 to 2.6); above s ≈ 10 the wavelet isolates the lowest Laplacian modes and its reliability collapses (α 0.72 → 0.37 by s = 53). Effective independent scales over the 16-point grid: 2.0–2.4 for every readout. There is about one decade of non-trivial, reliable diffusion content on these graphs, and inside it the readouts are strongly correlated with the raw FC. A whole-vector statistic cannot be scale-specific on this substrate.

**The construction that can.** Build the diffusion tree of the full rest_pre backbone at s = 1 (UPGMA of 1/ρ̂, the existing library tree). For each node pair, record k = the number of clusters remaining at the merge that joins them. Bin pairs by k into log₂ levels: k ∈ {2}, {3–4}, {5–8}, {9–16}, {17–32}, {33–64}, {> 64}. Levels are disjoint pair sets of 170–1100 pairs each, with same-shaft fraction 6–23 % (removed by the mask anyway). Within each level compute ρ_sym on a non-ultrametric readout, either the dense |ImCoh| or the heat kernel at a fixed s. The cophenetic vector itself cannot be used within levels because it is constant within a merge node (this is the ~117-value compression W0-C found). Scale specificity is then a statement about which levels carry the retained change, tested within patients.

**Today's numbers** (`level_decomposition.csv`, cross-shaft pairs, cohort medians):

| β, heat kernel s = 1 | k = 2 | 3–4 | 5–8 | 9–16 | 17–32 | 33–64 | > 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ρ_sym | 0.02 | −0.07 | 0.21 | 0.17 | 0.25 | 0.28 | 0.30 |
| ρ_sym / split-half reliability | 0.07 | −0.12 | 0.33 | 0.30 | 0.36 | 0.41 | 0.48 |

Within-patient trend (Spearman of level index vs ρ_sym, Wilcoxon one-sided across patients): β heat s = 1 p = 0.010 (9/10 positive), fine-minus-coarse contrast +0.30, p = 0.005; β raw p = 0.024; θ heat s = 4.7 p = 0.005; α flat (p 0.05–0.70); δ and high_γ flat. Robustness (`level_robustness.csv`): β raw p = 0.014–0.024 at every backbone fraction with the reference tree at s = 1, and 0.04–0.12 with the reference tree at s = 4.7; β heat p = 0.003–0.010 at f = 0.14 and 0.20, 0.138 at f = 0.07. The gradient does not change sign anywhere in the sweep.

**What this would mean if it survives.** The retained β change lives in the fine structure of the diffusion hierarchy (pairs that already communicate at short diffusion times) and is absent between the top-level modules. That is a scale-specific, cross-patient, hierarchy-referenced statement, in the units the LRG defines (modules remaining), and it does not depend on the reader believing that τ is comparable across implants.

**What kills it.** (a) The ordered sham shows the same level gradient (then it is a property of drift or of pair strength, not of the task). (b) Matched-strength surrogates rebuilt per level show it (then it is degree structure). (c) The gradient is a reliability artefact not removed by the ratio above; the cleanest check is to reference each level to its own sham, not to its reliability. (d) It disappears under knob integration over f ∈ [0.07, 0.20] and reference-tree scale.

**Lane M1 (scale specificity by hierarchy level).** Scope report first (`task-persistence-investigation/2026-09-08_hierarchy-level-decomposition.md`, written today), then: real arc and ordered sham per level, matched-strength per level, knob integration over f and reference scale, LOO, six bands. Compute: the real-arc part runs in seconds; matched-strength per level, timed today at 0.28 s per surrogate (117 nodes, 20·N(N−1)/2 swaps, strengths preserved to 1e-8), is 2.3 h single-core for 100 surrogates × 5 phases × 60 cells, under one hour on three workers; the sham rung uses the windowed cache of §4. Falsifier as above.

## 3. Objective 2 — band or spatial specificity

**Band.** The single paired β vs low_γ test was the wrong instrument at n = 10. The level construction gives each band a profile over seven levels rather than one number, and the profile shape is a within-patient quantity. The question becomes a level × band interaction: does β's gradient differ from α's flat profile? Today's probe says β and θ rise with level, α does not, δ and high_γ carry nothing. If that holds against the sham, the band statement is "β and θ traces are fine-level; α's is level-independent", which is a specificity claim that a repeated-measures test across six bands can carry. Report it with Friedman across bands on the fine-minus-coarse contrast, then post-hoc pairs, then LOO.

**Spatial.** The level construction localises for free: the fine-level pairs that carry the β trace belong to identifiable modules of the rest_pre tree, and each module has a Desikan-Killiany footprint. The July verdict that β has no fine home was reached on whole-vector node scores; a module-level statement ("the trace is carried by pairs inside these k ≈ 16–32 modules, whose anatomy is X") is a different object and has not been tested. Pool across patients by region using the existing `cohort_localization.py` machinery, resolution-preserving, whole-grid BH, LOO. What kills it: modules carrying the trace have no consistent anatomy across patients, which would return us to "delocalised" honestly.

## 4. Objective 3 — encoding vs inference

**What is closed, and why.** Block-level contrasts (learn vs test) at the whole-vector level are reproduced by a no-task arc in the correct temporal order (Lane E), and today's probe shows they do not separate by hierarchy level either: the level profiles of T_learn and T_test are the same, and their contrast is within ±0.05 at every level with sign counts of 2–8/10. Any statistic of the form f(learn) − f(test) on whole-block FC is confounded with temporal order by construction, and no regressor built from phases that share a term with the functional can fix it (the shared-D_B artefact).

**What the literature says the answer should look like (§6.2).** No electrophysiology study of transitive inference finds an inference state different in kind from premise encoding: monkey prefrontal recordings show graded modulation by symbolic distance and, in the one dataset that records both learning and test, a single low-dimensional manifold that progressively realigns from learning to test; human fMRI shows inference can be precomputed at encoding. Relational structure is built offline (Ellenbogen 2007), so the trace of the *learning* block in rest_post is the mechanistically expected one and the test block has no privileged status; at n = 89 the post-encoding rest state already resembles the encoding state more than the retrieval state (Folvik 2023). The honest re-statement of objective 3 is therefore not "find an inference-specific trace" but: **after time-equating, which block does rest_post reinstate, the learning configuration or the test configuration?** A learn > test answer is a dissociation, in the direction the literature predicts, and is publishable as offline consolidation reinstating the encoding configuration rather than the inference one. A learn = test answer is a task-general trace and the paper says so.

**The constructions, ranked by how well they defeat temporal order.** (1) *Time-equated cross-block similarity with an empirical drift kernel.* Similarity between windows of each task block and windows of rest_post as a function of their lag Δt, residualised against a drift kernel d(Δt) estimated from sliding-window pairs inside rest_pre and inside rest_post; the statistic is the residual learn→post similarity minus the residual test→post similarity, per band and per hierarchy level (§2). Null: sham arcs at matched lags plus matched-strength. This attacks drift directly instead of hoping specificity rescues it, and it subsumes the "deepen the sham" step (the drift kernel is the sham with many realizations). (2) *Within-rest_post lag profile, plateau vs decay.* The similarity of rest_post windows to the learning state as a function of position in rest_post, compared with the within-rest_pre similarity-versus-lag curve at identical lags and window lengths. Drift predicts monotone decay following the rest_pre kernel; a consolidation trace predicts a plateau or a non-monotone profile (flat reactivation over 30 min in Duan 2025). No learn/test contrast is needed and no markers. (3) *Difference-in-differences of within-block nonstationarity.* ΔFC(late − early) inside task_learn versus inside task_test versus inside equal-length rest windows, and the alignment of each with rest_post − rest_pre; learning should be nonstationary and aligned with the offline change, test and rest stationary. Weakest of the three: it isolates learning only, and block-onset transients mimic nonstationarity, so the first minute of every block is excluded.

**Cost.** All three need windowed |ImCoh| (Welch per window) on the two rest recordings and the two task blocks: no windowed cache exists (the July windowed work stored only embeddings). This is one compute job feeding constructions (1)–(3) and the drift kernel for §2's sham rung. Timed today on Pat_02 (117 channels, float32, nperseg 4096): one Welch pass on a 30 s window takes 1.6 s, and the complex coherency must be band-reduced on the fly because the full frequency-resolved array is 224 MB per window. Rest recordings are 8.4–11.2 min per phase; with the task blocks the cohort is roughly 40–60 min per patient, so 30 s windows at a 15 s step give ≈ 160–240 windows per patient, ≈ 5 min per patient and under one hour for the cohort single-core, three workers bring it to about 20 min. Lane E2 runs it once and evaluates all three.

## 5. Objective 4 — the SOZ marker

**Status after today's probe (2026-09-09, `feas_soz_marker.py`, `feas_soz_seeded_null.py`).** The July "detector at median 0.85" is not a detector: the score behind it is the seeded heat-kernel affinity, the mean of K_ij from node i to the clinically marked SOZ set, so it uses the labels it is scored against. No unsupervised node score in this pipeline separates SOZ contacts: strength (dense and backbone), degree, eigenvector centrality, PageRank, heat-kernel K_ii and the cross-shaft row sum at eight scales, and electrode density all sit at cohort median AUC 0.45–0.60 with at most 4/10 patients above 0.7, in both rest phases and all six bands.

**What the seeded number is made of.** Seeded affinity on rest_post at s = 10 reaches median AUC β 0.86 (10/10 above 0.7), δ 0.85, low_γ 0.81, θ 0.77, α 0.74. Random seed sets of the same size and the same per-shaft composition already score 0.68–0.70 median, 95th percentile 0.80–0.82, so the observed β and δ values sit just above the shaft-matched null (8/10 and 7/10 patients p < 0.05) while α, θ, high_γ do not. Removing same-shaft pairs before seeding collapses it: β 0.56 (shaft-matched null 0.45, 4/10), α 0.48, low_γ 0.55, high_γ 0.52, θ 0.61–0.66 (2/10). The only cross-shaft residual is δ: 0.62–0.67 against a null of 0.47–0.51 (95th percentile 0.59–0.65), 6–7/10 patients p < 0.05, median z 2.1–2.2, at s = 4.72 and s = 10. This agrees with the June off-shaft δ number (0.72, 8/10) that the memory index carried, and it is where the clinical content actually lives.

**What objective 4 can honestly become.** Not a marker that reads SOZ off the graph without labels. The defensible statement is that the clinically marked SOZ contacts form a cross-shaft δ diffusion community, band-specific (δ, not β or γ), beyond what shaft adjacency and set size produce. Two framings survive: (a) seed extension, the clinical use case: given a partial onset hypothesis, rank the remaining contacts; evaluated as leave-half-the-seeds-out AUC per band against the shaft-matched random-set null; (b) physiology meets pathology, now made precise: the β trace lives at hierarchy levels k ≥ 5 (§2) and the SOZ community lives in δ, so "the trace spares SOZ" becomes a pair-level overlap test between β trace-carrying pairs and δ SOZ-community pairs, with the same shaft-matched null.

**Lane L3, revised.** (i) Leave-half-seeds-out extension AUC per band, rest_pre and rest_post, cross-shaft only, shaft-matched random-set null with 200 draws (timed today: 10 s for 10 patients × 6 bands × 4 scales × 2 nulls). (ii) Matched-strength null on the δ community affinity, since the cross-shaft residual could still be strength structure. (iii) Overlap of β level-k ≥ 5 trace pairs with the δ SOZ community. (iv) External validation of (i) on ds004100 and ds003029 (§6.4), where onset channels and resection masks are both available, which lets the seed-extension framing be scored against resection rather than against the same clinician's labels. The spatial null question raised earlier is now moot: the shaft-matched seed null is a set-composition null, not a proximity null on |ImCoh|, and it is the one the data demanded.

### 6.1 What "a scale" means in the LRG, and why our τ axis was always going to be flat

In the LRG papers the scale-specific object is the entropic susceptibility C(τ) = −dS/d log τ of the density matrix ρ̂(τ) = e^{−τL}/Tr, and scales are counted by counting its peaks: Erdős–Rényi graphs "exhibit only a characteristic resolution scale", stochastic block models give two peaks, the structural human connectome a double-peaked transition (Villegas, Gili, Caldarelli, Gabrielli, Nat Phys 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07230; Villegas et al., PRR 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06669; Gabrielli et al., Nat Rev Phys 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12988). The admissible τ window is 1/λ_max ≲ τ ≲ 1/λ_2 (Villegas et al., PRR 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04514), i.e. log₁₀(λ_max/λ_2) decades, which for a 100-node graph at ~14 % density with a modular bottleneck is one to two decades. The only quantitative multi-scale statistic in the family is Nurisso et al.'s plateau length of C(τ) (Nat Phys 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11298), and Poggialini et al. (PRL 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19104) warn that the connectome "must be sparsified ... to reveal non-trivial scale-invariant features, but avoiding the trivial [transition] expected at the percolation critical point". Our audit reproduces all of this on our graphs: 2.1–2.4 decades on sparse backbones, one dominant C(τ) peak in most cells, and 1.1–1.5 effective independent scales of the kernel over the window. The LRG authors themselves would count at most two scales in our graphs. A continuous-τ sweep of whole-vector correlations has no precedent as a scale-resolved test in this literature.

No published LRG study tests a task or disease effect on functional brain graphs; the applications are structural connectomes without a condition contrast. The nearest precedent with a condition effect is Benigni et al. (Netw Neurosci 2021, https://direct.mit.edu/netn/article/5/3/831/102505), who computed the same density-matrix entropy on structural connectomes, split τ into micro / meso / macro bands, ran per-τ tests between healthy, MCI and AD with Bonferroni–Holm across τ and contrasts. Graph-signal-processing work on brain graphs resolves scale with a fixed few-band energy split common to all conditions (Preti & Van De Ville, Nat Commun 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07813; Griffa et al., NeuroImage 2022; Huang et al., Proc IEEE 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.01135), sign-randomised spectral surrogates as the null, and Bonferroni across bands × regions; scales that are independent by construction come from orthogonal dyadic subbands (Coifman & Maggioni, ACHA 2006) or tight frames (Behjat & Larsson 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09507). For hierarchical-level condition effects the rigorous construction is Jeub, Sporns & Fortunato (Sci Rep 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5818657/): a size-matched per-level permutation null, with the LRG's own criterion that a partition is significant only if stable across a window of neighbouring scales (Villegas et al., PRR 2025). Three constructions are therefore standard in the field for scale-resolved condition effects: (1) a fixed two- or few-band energy split with per-band tests; (2) orthogonal subbands tested per subband with Holm; (3) hierarchical-level tests with a size-matched per-level null and neighbouring-level stability. The level decomposition of §2 is construction (3); its per-level null is the ordered sham and matched-strength rebuilt per level, and its stability criterion is the sweep over backbone fraction and reference scale.

### 6.2 Transitive inference and offline reinstatement

Encoding vs inference. Di Bello et al. (Commun Biol 2024, https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07418-5): macaque dorsal PFC during the test phase only; β and γ anti-correlated within the trial, symbolic distance stretches the duration of the early β↓/γ↑ phase, a difference in degree. Brunamonti et al. (J Neurosci 2016) and Ramawat et al. (2022): dlPFC and PMd symbolic-distance and serial-position coding at test; novel pairs carry the same modulation types. The Ferraina-group dataset recording both learning and test (bioRxiv 2024, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.29.620924v1.full): one one-dimensional "mental line" manifold that rotates continuously from learning through early to late test, not an orthogonal state. Human scalp EEG (Cereb Cortex 2024, https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/34/1/bhad494/7477786): unseen > seen pairs give frontal θ power, θ–α 1:2 phase locking and weak fronto-parietal α coherence, read by the authors as quantitative. fMRI: Berens & Bird (PLOS Comput Biol 2022) find hippocampal and mPFC pattern similarity tracking hierarchical distance with nothing inference-specific; Koster et al. (Neuron 2018) find the one mechanism dissociable in kind, deep-to-superficial entorhinal recirculation, laminar and band-free; Zeithamova & Preston (J Neurosci 2010; Neuron 2012) show encoding-phase hippocampal and vmPFC activity predicts later inference, i.e. inference can be precomputed at encoding. The within-trial β/γ, θ and θ–α signatures average out in whole-phase FC; the relational-structure signature is representational, not connectivity.

Offline consolidation. Ellenbogen et al. (PNAS 2007, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1863467/): first-order inference 52 % at 20 min → 70–74 % after 12–24 h with premise retention unchanged, second-order 93 % after sleep vs 70 % wake; relational structure is built offline. Tambini & Davachi (PNAS 2013): encoding multivoxel structure more similar to post-encoding rest than to baseline, with elapsed time equated between comparisons and a second task as control. Schapiro et al. (Nat Commun 2018): item-specific reinstatement, no pre-rest baseline, label-scrambling null. Folvik et al. (Cereb Cortex 2023, n = 89): hippocampal–neocortical rest connectivity rises after encoding and the post-rest state resembles the encoding state (ρ = 0.43) more than retrieval, without tracking memory. Halpern et al. (Nat Neurosci 2025, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01884-8; iEEG n = 215): spectral representational similarity between item encoding and inter-stimulus intervals, recency controlled by first-half items versus later ISIs, item specificity does the rest. Zavecz et al. (Cortex 2024): wPLI similarity sim(learn, post) − sim(learn, pre), α and δ, no time-order control; the closest analogue to our pipeline and it carries our confound unaddressed. Duan et al. (Prog Neurobiol 2025; iEEG n = 11): object-specific reactivation in 30-min rest, flat across the rest period, predicts recall. Drift is real and directional (Manning 2011; Folkerts 2018; Wang et al., Nat Commun 2025: entorhinal and anterior-hippocampal connectivity similarity falls with interval).

Marker-free designs. No accepted method separates encoding from inference without trial markers. Nearest precedents: Henin et al. (Sci Adv 2021), within-block emergence of a hidden structure plus a structure-free control stream; time-equated comparisons (Tambini 2013; Halpern 2025). The "orthogonal inference state" argument is supported only at circuit level.

Publication precedents 2022–2026. Every intracranial paper at Nature Neuroscience or Neuron without behaviour is carried by single units or laminar recordings (Staresina 2023, n = 10 patients, sleep; Hayat 2022), a state manipulation (Zelmann 2023), stimulation plus modelling (Nat Commun 2025), cross-modal validation, or a clinical link (Sheybani 2023). There is no precedent for n ≈ 10 band-limited whole-phase FC with no behaviour; the closest analogues went to Cortex and Progress in Neurobiology. This is the sentence that sets the ceiling: **without a clinical link or a mechanism, the trace alone is not a Nature Neuroscience paper, whatever its statistics.** The clinical link is objective 4.

### 6.3 SOZ markers: benchmarks and what a reviewer demands

Published interictal-FC SOZ markers that report patient-held-out AUC land at 0.65–0.86; the 0.88–0.94 figures come from pooled node-level cross-validation or region-level models without hold-out, which reviewers at Brain and Annals now discount (Pilet et al., Sci Rep 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12098728/, show the 0.91 → 0.73 drop explicitly; Balaji & Parhi, Sci Rep 2025, subject-independent 0.86; Jiang et al., Ann Neurol 2022, 0.94 pooled; Narasimhan et al., Epilepsia 2020, 0.88 region-level; Goodale et al., Neurosurgery 2020, α ImCoh 0.78). Our July leave-one-patient-out median of 0.85 sits at the top of the held-out range, unverified. The one control that decides whether a marker is real is a spatial null: electrode density and anatomical location alone give AUC 0.70, and adding FC did not improve it significantly in 110 patients (Conrad et al., J Neural Eng 2022, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9590099/). Whether the SOZ is a hub or an island is measure-dependent: undirected coherence and strength make it hub-like, causal and outflow centralities make it a sink (the "interictal suppression" picture of Narasimhan 2020, Jiang 2022, Gunnarsdottir et al., Brain 2022, Doss et al., Brain 2024), and once electrode density is corrected SOZ connectivity is lower than the contralateral homologue (Conrad 2022). No published node-level SOZ marker uses communicability, heat-kernel or Laplacian-eigenvector scales; the nearest is global Laplacian synchronizability λ₂/λ_max at outcome level (Kini et al., Brain 2019). A reviewer will note that e^{−τL} interpolates between strength (τ → 0) and the Fiedler partition (τ → ∞) and will ask the τ ≈ 10/λ_max peak to beat both endpoints and the standard spectral centralities. Neural fragility reached Nature Neuroscience with n = 91 across five centres, surgical outcome as ground truth, and a head-to-head against 20 baselines (Li et al., Nat Neurosci 2021, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8547387/); the field's current standards ask for separate validation cohorts, outcome, spatial-sampling and referencing awareness and open data (Bernabei et al., Brain 2023). With n = 10 and no outcome, the realistic route is external replication on the open cohorts released with those papers (OpenNeuro ds003029, ds003876, ds004100; more than 100 patients), which is being scoped in §6.4.

Physiology meets pathology: task networks appear to spare the SOZ (the θ-coherence memory network is absent in SOZ electrodes, Young et al., NeuroImage 2018; SOZ contacts "do not participate" in the encoding θ network, Saboo et al., Epilepsia 2021), cognitive damage is driven by spikes outside the SOZ (Ung et al., Brain 2017), and no study has tested whether task-induced resting-state connectivity changes spare the SOZ. That claim would be the first of its kind and needs the same spatial null.

Ranked reviewer demands for a marker paper: (1) electrode-density spatial null with AUC of null, marker and marker-over-null; (2) head-to-head on identical nodes and folds against strength, degree, eigenvector centrality, communicability/PageRank, band power, and the τ → 0 / τ → ∞ endpoints of our own kernel; (3) patient-held-out validation with per-patient AUC distributions, never pooled node AUC as the headline; (4) referencing sensitivity and same-shaft / distance control; (5) spike-free epochs and several interictal segments; (6) outcome for at least a subset, or the claim restricted to "agreement with the clinician SOZ" in line one; (7) external replication on an open cohort; (8) open code and data. Item (1) conflicts with a standing project rule (no spatial or proximity null on |ImCoh|), which was written for the trace, not for the marker; lifting it for Lane L3 is the user's call and is flagged in §7.

### 6.4 Open cohorts for external validation

Scoped 2026-09-09 from the OpenNeuro GitHub mirrors (README level; sampling rate, reference scheme, licence and awake-rest duration per subject still have to be read from the `_ieeg.json` sidecars before any commitment). ds004100 (HUP, 58 patients, ECoG and sEEG, interictal and ictal clips, clinically marked seizure-onset channels and resection/ablation overlap channels, ICBM152 MNI coordinates, Bernabei et al. Brain 2022) is the first target: onset labels plus resection masks make the seed-extension framing scorable against a surgical outcome rather than against the labeller. ds003029 (multi-centre fragility set, 80 of 100 patients public, ECoG and sEEG, ictal snapshots and interictal runs, early-onset and spread annotations, resection from post-operative MRI, sEEG electrode layouts, Li et al. Nature Neuroscience 2021) is the second. ds003876 (same consortium, interictal only, three centres, sleep/awake annotated) is the interictal complement if ds003029 interictal runs are too short. ds005398 (185 paediatric patients, UCLA and Detroit, interictal sleep, resection status and outcome in participants.tsv, HFO derivatives) is outcome-rich but sleep-only and paediatric, so it is a stress test, not a replication. ds004752 (15 patients, scalp plus depth, Sternberg working-memory task, MNI coordinates, no SOZ labels, Boran et al. 2019/2020) is the only open task set with a per-trial structure and is relevant to objective 3, not 4; it has no rest-pre/task/rest-post design. The MNI Open iEEG Atlas gives normal-tissue reference spectra, not connectivity, and is not usable here. OpenNeuro datasets are CC0 unless stated; the ds003029 Cleveland Clinic subset needs a separate agreement.

## 7. Order of work

1. **Lane M1** — scale specificity by hierarchy level: real arc per level, matched-strength per level, knob-integrated over f and reference scale, six bands, LOO. Runs in minutes and decides whether objective 1 exists at the level of independence nulls. Its drift rung comes from item 2.
2. **Windowed |ImCoh| compute (one job)** — sliding windows on rest_pre, rest_post, task_learn, task_test for all patients and bands. Feeds the drift kernel for M1's sham rung, the deepened sham for the persistence result (q = 0.146 today), and all three constructions of Lane E2. Estimated today at under one hour single-core for the cohort (§4).
3. **Lane L3** — SOZ marker re-derivation with level axis and the reviewer baselines (§6.3); the trace-spares-SOZ test rides on M1's output. Needs one decision from the user: the electrode-density spatial null that reviewers demand for a marker is currently barred by the project rule "no spatial / proximity null on |ImCoh|", which was written for the trace. Recommendation: lift it for L3 only.
4. **Lane E2** — the three time-equated constructions of §4 on the windowed cache; the answer is "learn", "test", or "task-general", and each is written up as such.
5. **Lane B1** — band × level interaction and module-level localisation, on M1's output with M1's nulls.
6. **External validation of the marker** on the open cohort(s) of §6.4, if L3 stands. This is the step that turns a pilot into a paper reviewers can accept.
7. Optional: amplitude-envelope FC as a mechanistic contrast, only if M1 and L3 stand.

Only after 1–3 is there anything to write. The results and methods sections are not touched until then.

## 8. What this plan does not promise

The level gradient is a same-day probe on one readout family with no null. The ordered sham could reproduce it. The within-block design could return nothing, in which case the encoding/inference claim is dropped and replaced by "task-general". The SOZ marker could fall against strength and power baselines. If all three fail, the honest paper is the persistence trace at α/β against independence nulls plus whatever the marker survives, and it is not a Nature Neuroscience paper. The plan is built so that each of those outcomes is known within days, not weeks.
