## Head

Scale is τ. The object of objective 1 is the profile of the cross-phase trace along the spectral window of each network, s = τλ_max ∈ [1, λ_max/λ_2], and the multiscale feature is what that profile shows beyond a null that keeps every single-scale quantity (edge weights, node strengths, the raw pair-level trace) and removes only the network's organisation. This page fixes the graph, the operator, the readouts, the statistic, the nulls, the aggregation and the pass criteria before any run.

## 1. The graph: significance-sparsified |ImCoh| per phase

Edge weight: w_ij = ⟨|Im C_ij(f)|⟩_{f∈band}, imaginary part of coherency per Welch bin then band average (Nolte 2004; Ewald 2012; Bastos and Schoffelen 2016), from the existing frequency-resolved cache. The imaginary part removes zero-lag mixing (volume conduction, common reference) by construction; no bipolar re-referencing is used because it changes the node set and the anatomy of every contact.

Sparsification by significance, not by fraction. For each patient and phase, K univariate surrogates of the time series are built by Fourier phase randomisation of every channel independently (autospectra preserved exactly, cross-channel phase relations destroyed; Theiler 1992 for the univariate surrogate, Prichard and Theiler 1994 for why the multivariate version must not be used here: it preserves the cross-spectrum and therefore the coherence). Each surrogate goes through the same Welch recipe, giving a null distribution of w_ij under "no coupling beyond the autospectra" for every pair and band. An edge is kept when its observed w_ij exceeds the null at a false-discovery rate q over all pairs of the graph (Benjamini–Hochberg; q = 0.05 primary, 0.01 and 0.10 reported), and it keeps its weight w_ij; nothing is binarised. Same-shaft pairs stay in the graph (they carry real coupling and the diffusion needs them) but are excluded from every pair-level statistic (probe-bias guide). If the kept graph is disconnected, the maximum spanning tree is added back, weights unchanged, and the number of added edges is reported. Density becomes a measured variable per patient, band and phase, reported in a table, never a knob.

Is this sellable in Nature Neuroscience? Yes, and it is closer to what a connectivity reviewer asks for than any proportional threshold. Surrogate-based edge significance with FDR control on intracranial recordings is the Kramer, Eden, Cash and Kolaczyk (2009, Phys. Rev. E) protocol, used on ECoG/sEEG in Kramer et al. (2011, J. Neurosci.) and Chu et al. (2012, J. Neurosci.) for persistent functional networks, and phase-randomised surrogates for coherence significance are standard in the ImCoh literature itself. Proportional thresholding is the practice under attack (van den Heuvel et al. 2017, NeuroImage; Garrison et al. 2015) because it forces equal density across groups and phases and then compares topology. The two things a reviewer will ask are answered by construction: the null preserves each channel's spectrum, so significance is not a power effect; and the density per phase is reported, so a phase difference in density is a finding, not a confound hidden by a fixed fraction. The one real risk is empirical, not methodological: on 10-minute recordings the no-coupling floor of |ImCoh| is about 0.6/√n_seg ≈ 0.03–0.04, so most pairs may pass and the graph may stay dense in some bands. The probe in §8 measures this on two patients before anything is built; if a band keeps more than about 80% of its pairs the protocol still holds (the weights carry the structure) but the paper must say so, and an effect-size floor cannot be added without reintroducing a knob.

## 2. The operator and the scale window

Combinatorial Laplacian L = D − W on the significance-sparsified weighted graph, ρ̂(τ) = e^{−τL}/Tr e^{−τL}, eigendecomposition once per graph. Scale s = τλ_max. The LRG window is set by the spectrum of each graph: from τ = 1/λ_max (s = 1, the raw graph barely diffused) to τ = 1/λ_2 (s = λ_max/λ_2, only the Fiedler mode left). No fixed numeric range: the grid is log-spaced on that window, per graph, with G = 21 points, and every profile is reported in the normalised coordinate u = log s / log(λ_max/λ_2) ∈ [0, 1] so that patients with different implants and different spectral gaps are aligned on their own windows. Both λ_max/λ_2 and the u grid are reported per patient, band and phase. When the four phases of one cell have different windows, the cross-phase statistic at u uses each phase's own s(u); this is the only way to compare trees at the same relative depth of diffusion.

Readouts at scale s, three of them, primary first: (i) cophenetic distances of the UPGMA tree of 1/ρ̂(s), the canonical LRG pipeline, ultrametric; (ii) the pair vector ρ̂_ij(s), non-ultrametric; (iii) the graph wavelet −τ ∂ρ̂/∂τ at s, band-pass in the Laplacian spectrum, the non-cumulative readout. (i) is the claim's readout; (ii) and (iii) are reported to show whether the tree adds anything over the kernel and whether the cumulative kernel hides a band-limited effect.

## 3. The statistic

Trace: ρ_sym(u) = ½[Spearman(D_task − D_A, D_post − D_B) + Spearman(D_task − D_B, D_post − D_A)], with A and B the contiguous halves of rest_pre, D the readout at scale u, over cross-shaft pairs. Not zero-centred; only differences from a null and shapes across u are interpreted. Objective 3 uses the same functional with task_learn in place of task_test on whole blocks, duration entered as a covariate across patients (no truncation), and the contrast profile ρ_sym^test(u) − ρ_sym^learn(u).

The multiscale feature. Let ρ_sym^N(u) be the same statistic on the joint null of §4. The multiscale excess is e(u) = ρ_sym(u) − mean_N ρ_sym^N(u). By construction e(u → 0) = 0: the null keeps the raw trace exactly, so nothing at the raw limit counts. The claim "the trace is multiscale" means e(u) > 0 in a window of u; the claim "scale-specific" means that window is bounded away from u = 1 as well, and its location is aligned across patients. No single scale is the feature: the feature is the profile, and it is reported as the profile (no scalar collapse, no scale-max).

## 4. The nulls, and what each rejects

N1, joint strength-preserving rewiring (primary, to be implemented). Draw a sequence of 4-cycle swaps (a,b),(c,d) → (a,c),(b,d) once per surrogate and apply the same sequence, with the same transferred weight δ, to all four (five, with task_learn) phase matrices; δ is bounded by the minimum over phases of the two source weights so all matrices stay non-negative. Consequences, exact: every phase keeps its node strengths; every pair keeps its cross-phase differences w_X(ij) − w_Y(ij), hence D_task − D_A and D_post − D_B at the raw level are unchanged and raw ρ_sym is preserved to machine precision; the sparsity pattern, loops, communities and spectrum are destroyed progressively with the number of swaps. What it rejects: "the τ dependence of the trace is what any network with these strengths and this raw trace would show". What it cannot reject: "the multiscale organisation exists but is not task-specific" (that is N3) and "the trace is a generic state change" (behaviour would be needed).

N2, per-phase matched-strength rewiring (the existing library null). Independent rewiring per phase destroys the pair correspondence, so ρ_sym^N ≈ 0 at every u; it only certifies that a trace exists beyond strengths. Reported once as a floor, never used for a scale argument (user, 2026-09-09).

N3, ordered sham for task-specificity. Whole-phase durations only: the 2-minute chunk version is below the noise floor (lane_m1_02). With one task per patient this is limited to the drift comparison rest_pre halves versus rest_post halves, which is confounded by the task being directional (drift memory); it is reported as a bound, not as a null of the claim.

Time-series surrogates (§1) are the null of the graph, not of the trace; they enter only in edge selection.

## 5. Aggregation across patients

Every patient contributes one profile e(u) per band and readout; all ten are shown, none dropped. Cohort test at each u: two-sided Wilcoxon on e(u) across patients with leave-one-patient-out maximum p; the window W_b of a band is the maximal contiguous u-interval where p < 0.05 holds and survives LOO. Alignment: the per-patient argmax u* of e(u) and its interquartile range, compared with the IQR of u* obtained from the N1 surrogate profiles resampled ten at a time (p_aligned). Band comparison: Friedman test on the window-integrated excess ∫_W e(u) du across the six bands, followed by the ranking; "band-selective" means the Friedman rejects and one band's window is non-empty while others' are empty or unaligned. Implant heterogeneity is absorbed by the u coordinate and by each patient's own null; density, N and λ_max/λ_2 are tabulated and regressed against the per-patient window-integrated excess so a density-driven profile is visible.

## 6. Pass criteria, written before the run

Objective 1 passes if, for at least one band, W_b is non-empty, bounded away from u = 0 and u = 1, aligned (p_aligned < 0.05) and stable across q ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10}; it fails as "scale-specific" if e(u) > 0 at more than 90% of the grid (multiscale but uniform), and fails outright if no window survives LOO. Objective 2 passes if the Friedman test rejects and the windows differ by band in location or presence. Objective 3 passes if the contrast profile test−learn has a window under the same criteria with duration regressed out; otherwise it is reported as not separable in this dataset. Objective 4 is not on the τ axis: the SOZ result stands as the cross-shaft δ community (plan §5) and is linked to the trace only through the overlap test defined there.

## 7. What is not claimed

No behaviour, so no link from the window to performance. No causal role of τ; the window is a descriptive location in the network's own spectral coordinates. No "micro/meso/macro" labels: a window is quoted as a u-range and, where useful, as a cluster count of the tree at that s. No result on the dense graph, whose kernel has a single scale by construction, and none on a proportional backbone.

## 8. Order of work and compute

(a) Significance-density probe on two patients (running 2026-09-09; `feasibility/feas_significance_density.py`) to know whether the protocol sparsifies; numbers to be inserted below. (b) Surrogate-based edge selection for 10 patients × 5 phases × 6 bands: K = 100 phase-randomised surrogates per patient-phase, one Welch pass each (about 20 s per pass on 117 channels, so 100 × 50 passes ≈ 28 h single-core, 3.5 h on 8 workers; the per-pair null is Gaussian enough that K = 40 may do, to be checked on the probe), written as a cache with per-pair p-values so q can be changed without recompute. (c) N1 implementation in the library with two unit tests: strengths preserved per phase to 1e-8, raw ρ_sym preserved to 1e-12. (d) The τ-profile run: 60 cells × 200 N1 surrogates × 21 scales × 3 readouts, about 0.5 s per surrogate, 100 min on 8 workers. (e) Figures: per-patient e(u) profiles per band, cohort window, alignment; then objective 3.

Probe results (§8a, 2026-09-09, rest_pre, 10 phase-randomised surrogates, cross-shaft pairs, `data/paper_final/feasibility/significance_density_Pat_02.csv` and `_Pat_13.csv`). The protocol sparsifies, and the density it leaves is band- and patient-dependent. Fraction of pairs above the pooled null 99th percentile (a proxy for q ≈ 0.01 before FDR adaptation): Pat_02 δ 0.55, θ 0.70, α 0.69, β 0.61, low_γ 0.48, high_γ 0.17; Pat_13 δ 0.46, θ 0.53, α 0.44, β 0.33, low_γ 0.10, high_γ 0.007. Above the 95th percentile: Pat_02 0.27–0.80, Pat_13 0.01–0.68. The no-coupling floor is 0.023–0.027 (median) with a 99th percentile of 0.03–0.055, as the 0.6/√n_seg estimate predicted. Two consequences for the paper. First, the graphs of δ–β keep 30–70% of pairs, loop-rich and far from a backbone, with density now a measured quantity that differs by band and by patient; the τ window λ_max/λ_2 will differ accordingly, which is what the u coordinate is for. Second, high_γ (80–300 Hz) carries no |ImCoh| coupling above the autospectral floor in either patient (Pat_13 observed median below the null median), and low_γ is marginal in Pat_13 (10%); on this protocol high_γ has no graph to diffuse on and leaves the LRG analysis by construction, and low_γ must be reported with its density. Every earlier high_γ statement in this project was made on a proportional backbone that kept 14–20% of pairs regardless of significance. Ten surrogates give a coarse per-pair null; K = 40–100 is needed for per-pair p-values and BH, and the observed-versus-null z (median 3.7–5.2 in δ–β for Pat_02, 1.3–3.5 for Pat_13) says K = 40 will resolve q = 0.05 comfortably for those bands.
