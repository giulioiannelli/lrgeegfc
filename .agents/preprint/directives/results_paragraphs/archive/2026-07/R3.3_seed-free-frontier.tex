\paragraph{Without seeds, the marker does not transfer across patients.}
This detector needs seeds --- a few known seizure contacts to define the target the affinity is measured against --- and the version that would matter most clinically needs none. On this cohort, the seed-free version does not work. Read from each contact's intrinsic diffusion properties alone --- self-return, slow-mode localization, cophenetic persistence --- fused across bands and transferred across patients, the same features that reach \(0.81\) with seeds fall to an \gls{auc} of \(0.53\), at their label-shuffle floor (\(0.43\)); the only seed-free feature that transfers across patients is electrode depth (\(0.69\)) --- and that is not a connectivity signal but the implantation geometry itself: shafts are placed so that their deep contacts reach the tissue already suspected of seizure onset, so depth tracks the surgeon's target, not the network, and adding the diffusion features to it only lowers the score (\(0.69 \to 0.63\)). Label-free community selection, single-phase routing, and cross-phase routing rigidity fail the same way --- five independent seed-free tests, each sitting at the strength or shuffle floor. The reason is mechanistic: the discriminating information is relational, a contact's affinity to \emph{known} seizure tissue, and without that anchor the seizure community is not separable from ordinary diffusion communities. A seed-free localizer therefore remains open; the result established here is the seed-based read-out.
