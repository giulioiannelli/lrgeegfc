# `task_learn` is clean — the duration asymmetry is *relative*, not a quality problem

**Head.** The duration confound that complicated the inference story comes entirely
from `task_test` being **longer** than `task_learn`, not from `task_learn` being
**bad**. Every `task_learn` recording is artifact-free (0 % missing samples) and,
even at its shortest, long enough to estimate the connectivity reliably. So
`task_learn` is a sound encoding reference for all ten patients; no patient should
be dropped or down-weighted for learn-phase quality. This closes the N2 §G
"`task_learn` length/quality audit" to-do.

## Numbers (cohort n = 10)

- **Missing data:** 0.00 % NaN in *both* `task_learn` and `task_test` for **all
  ten** patients. No corruption, no gaps.
- **`task_learn` duration:** median **720 s** (≈ 12 min), range **431–837 s**.
- **`task_test` duration:** median **1098 s** (≈ 18 min), range **1045–1260 s**.
- **Ratio (test / learn):** median **1.57×**, range **1.35–2.53×**. The extreme is
  **Pat_06** (learn 431 s, test 1091 s → 2.53×); the mildest is **Pat_02** (1.35×).
- **Estimation power:** at nperseg = 4096 (2.0 s windows, 50 % overlap, 2048 Hz),
  even the **shortest** `task_learn` (Pat_06, 431 s) affords **~430 Welch
  segments**; the median patient affords **~720**. Hundreds of segments is ample
  for a stable band-averaged `|ImCoh|` connectivity estimate.

## What this means for the duration confound

- The asymmetry is **purely relative**: `task_test` has ~1.6× more data than
  `task_learn`, but `task_learn` is *not* under-powered in absolute terms. So the
  confound is "one phase sharper than the other," not "one phase unreliable."
- This is exactly the asymmetry the duration controls addressed — and it is why the
  **clean** control (per-patient regression of the effect against the test/learn
  ratio) is the right instrument: the ratio is a meaningful, well-defined per-patient
  number (1.35–2.53×), and the β effect does **not** scale with it.
- **No new dropout.** `task_learn` quality does not justify excluding any patient.
  Pat_06 is the largest ratio and worth keeping an eye on in any per-patient table,
  but its 431 s / ~430 segments are still well within the reliable regime.

## Caveats

- This audits **length and missing-data**, not stationarity or electrode-level SNR
  per contact; those were not separately interrogated here (the matched-strength
  null and the cross-phase design absorb stationary nuisance structure, but a
  per-contact SNR pass remains an option if a reviewer presses).
- Pat_03 is 1024 Hz (handled at the config layer); its `task_learn` (837 s, the
  longest) is fully adequate.
