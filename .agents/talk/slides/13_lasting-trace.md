4. SPEECH  (~80 s)

Did the task leave a lasting trace? Yes. Read the resting hierarchy afterwards and it doesn't snap back — its tree still looks like the task's.

Now read it multiscale. [Fig 1 — the gate map] Every band down the side, diffusion scale from fine to coarse across the bottom, and the colour is how strongly the rest matches the task, measured against a matched-strength null — grey means it never beats the null. Beta is a solid bright block across the entire width: it clears the null at every single scale, sixteen out of sixteen — scale-invariant. Alpha lights up as a broad mesoscale band. Theta and low-gamma stay grey — those are the built-in controls, they never trace. So the trace is real, multiscale, and selective: the brain doesn't reorganize everything, it reorganizes beta and alpha.

And it isn't one lucky patient. [Fig 2 — the violins] For each band I take its strongest and its weakest scale and show all ten patients as distance above their own null, zero being the null. A filled dot means that scale clears the matched-strength gate; an open ring means it doesn't. Just count the filled dots. Beta has two — it clears the gate even at its weakest scale, that's what scale-invariant means. Alpha, delta, high-gamma have one — they trace only where they're strongest. Theta and low-gamma have none — two open rings, no trace, even though their median can sit a hair above the line. That's the whole point: above the null line is not the same as clearing the null. The referee, everywhere, is matched-strength, read scale by scale.

So: a lasting trace, carried by beta and alpha, beta the only one that holds at every scale. What it can't yet tell you is where in the brain, or whether simpler connectivity sees it too — that's next.
